## Root Cause Analysis

### The cascade failure

Everything traces back to **Earth→FG3** landing in the wrong local minimum:

1. Earth→FG3: starting from (T_d=0, T_t=Hohmann≈3.28 TU), our NLP finds T_t≈4.41, dv≈10.19 km/s. The **paper finds T_t≈6.26, dv=9.51 km/s from the same starting conditions.**
2. Our Earth→FG3 T_t=4.41 → FG3 arrives at T_d≈4.47. Paper's T_t=6.26 → FG3 arrives at T_d≈6.35.
3. FG3→Bennu T_d_min = 4.47 (ours) vs 6.35 (paper). At T_d=4.47 with T_t=Hohmann≈2.4, NLP finds the 11.43 km/s local minimum. The paper at T_d=6.35 finds 7.32 km/s.
4. Bennu→Earth cascades from that.

### Why Earth→FG3 finds different local minima

Starting from (T_d=0, T_t=Hohmann≈3.28), our trust-constr solver converges to T_t≈4.41. The paper converges to T_t≈6.26. **Same starting conditions, different solver result.** This is a numerical/implementation difference between our scipy trust-constr and whatever the paper used (likely MATLAB's trust-region). The gradient at (0, 3.28) in the Δv landscape apparently points differently.

Our `compute_delta_v` is verified correct against paper Table 5 values. The difference is the NLP's numerical gradient direction, not the underlying trajectory computation.

---

## What This Means for Reproduction

The paper's algorithm is **not fully reproducible** with scipy trust-constr starting from Hohmann because the solver finds different local minima for Earth→FG3. The paper's result depends on their specific solver implementation converging to T_t≈6.26 from T_t≈3.28, which ours does not.

The `VRTPP_PR_Optimization.ipynb` grid scan (Bug 13 fix) was the correct engineering solution: it scans T_t values at the actual T_d_min and finds the right basin regardless of Hohmann. That approach does reproduce the paper's delta-v values.

## What to Investigate Next Session

### Option 1: Accept the limitation
PaperModel is the faithful implementation of what the paper describes. The fact that it doesn't reproduce the paper's exact result is a documented finding — the paper's algorithm is solver-dependent. Note this in any writeup.

### Option 2: Investigate NLP solver settings
The difference between T_t=4.41 and T_t=6.26 from (T_d=0, T_t=3.28) comes from gradient direction. Try:
- Different NLP tolerances (loosen gtol/xtol to see if solver explores further)
- Different NLP method (`SLSQP` or `L-BFGS-B` instead of `trust-constr`)
- Multiple restarts from a few T_t starting points for just the first iteration

### Option 3: Hybrid approach for PaperModel
Use the grid scan ONLY for iteration 1 (first call), and then the paper's T_t-from-previous-iteration for all subsequent calls. This matches the spirit of the paper (use a good starting point) while not introducing the T_d=0 vs large T_d_min mismatch problem. This is essentially what VRTPP_PR_Optimization.ipynb does with the Bug 13 fix.

### Remaining non-paper-faithful item
T_d_max = T_d_min + 5.0 cap in `optimize_segment`. The paper has no upper bound on T_d beyond T_d_min. The paper's FG3 departure at T_d=8.83 vs T_d_min=6.35 requires 2.45 TU of waiting — within the 5 TU window, so not blocking. But longer waits would require increasing this.
