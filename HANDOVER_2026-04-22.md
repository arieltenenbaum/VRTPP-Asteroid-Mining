**Change B — NLP warm-start for first-seen arcs (commits `7053566` → reverted same session)**  
First attempt: pass init NLP's T_t (from `init_times`) as warm-start for first-seen arcs in iteration 1 of the iterative loop. Reasoning: "previous iteration" T_t could mean the init step's result.

**This caused a new failure:** Running the notebook produced:
- FG3→Bennu: 10.32 km/s (still wrong, now due to different T_d_min context)
- Ryugu→CC21: **40.92 km/s** (catastrophically wrong)
- 2-spacecraft solution converging in 2 iterations to a bad fixed point

**Root cause of the failure:** Init T_t is found at T_d=0. The iterative NLP applies that T_t as warm-start at T_d_min which can be 10–25 TU for later arcs (e.g., Ryugu→CC21 at T_d≈20 TU). At a completely different T_d, the init's T_t puts the NLP in a wrong basin — hence 40.92 km/s. The 2-iteration convergence was a false convergence: the NLP found the same bad local minima in both iterations (Δv change = 0), triggering the hard criterion.

**Change C — NLP warm-start reverted to Hohmann (current state)**  
Cell 24 (`solve_vrtpp_pr`): Removed `init_times` lookup entirely. For first-seen arcs, T_t_prev_val = None, which causes `optimize_segment` to default to T_t_hoh (Hohmann). This is the literal paper description:

> *"The initial guess for the departure and transfer times in the NLP is set to the lower bound specified in Eq. (44) and the transfer time from the previous iteration, respectively."*

For iteration 1, there is no "previous iteration" T_t — Hohmann is the natural and paper-implicit default.

**Change D — Soft convergence removed (commit `7053566`, kept)**

## Open Problem: NLP Local Minimum for FG3→Bennu

The fundamental unresolved issue is that the paper's algorithm relies on luck: the Hohmann warm-start happens to land in the right T_t basin for each arc in the paper's specific 3-arc solution. Whether this reproduces on our hardware/solver depends on the gradient landscape at those starting conditions.

**Key diagnostic to run next session:** Does iteration 1 give:
- Earth→FG3: ~9.51 km/s (T_t≈6.26)?  
- FG3→Bennu: ~7.32 km/s (T_t≈7.06)?  
- Bennu→Earth: ~8.17 km/s?

If Earth→FG3 finds T_t≈6.26 instead of T_t≈4.43 (as previous runs showed), then the cascade into FG3→Bennu is correct and we may reproduce the paper.

If Earth→FG3 still finds T_t≈4–5 TU (different local minimum), the cascade fails and FG3→Bennu will not reproduce 7.32 km/s.

**Why Earth→FG3 matters so much:**  
The paper finds Earth→FG3 at T_t=6.26 TU (long transfer). Starting at T_d=0, T_t=Hohmann≈3.28 TU, the trust-constr solver may or may not climb to T_t≈6.26. Our previous run found T_t≈4.43 — a different local minimum at the same starting conditions. This suggests a subtle difference in the Δv landscape or solver behavior vs the paper.

### 2. If Earth→FG3 finds the wrong basin
The Hohmann time for Earth→FG3 is ≈3.28 TU (semi-major axis average). At (T_d=0, T_t=3.28), the gradient may point to T_t≈4–5 rather than T_t≈6.26. Options:
- Check Fig 2a of the paper: where is the paper's selected trajectory relative to Hohmann?
- Try different ε or solver settings
- Accept the local minimum and note it in the paper comparison

### 3. If the solution doesn't match the paper
The paper's algorithm is inherently case-specific. The 2-spacecraft solution with objective 27.2 from the previous run may actually represent a higher-objective solution than the paper found (if the delta-v values were computed correctly). The paper itself acknowledges it finds local minima, not global optima.

### 4. Remaining non-paper-faithful item
The T_d_max = T_d_min + 5.0 cap in `optimize_segment` is still present and not in the paper. The paper has no upper bound on T_d. Removing it could allow the NLP to find lower-Δv trajectories that require waiting longer at a body (e.g., the paper's FG3 wait of 2.45 TU is within range, but longer waits would require increasing this cap).
