### Where it deviates from the paper

**1. Initialization (Sec. IV.A) — partial deviation (required by platform)**

The paper states: *"solve the trajectory optimization problem for each pair of bodies using zero departure time and the Hohmann transfer time as the initial guess."*

`VRTPP-PaperModel.ipynb` does this plus a 7-point T_t scan at T_d=0 (candidates {1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13} TU) before running the NLP from (T_d=0, T_t=Hohmann). The scan is a practical necessity on macOS/M2 where the Hohmann starting point (T_t≈3.27 TU for Earth→FG3) converges to a wrong basin (10.4 km/s vs paper's 9.51 km/s). Without the scan, initialization produces infeasible mass ratios that prevent the MILP from finding any valid route (see §7, Gap 1 and Gap 2 for full analysis).

The scan stays within the paper's zero-departure-time intent. The NLP is still run from (T_d=0, T_t=Hohmann) as a fallback if it beats the scan best.

**2. Porkchop plots (Fig. 2) — missing**

The paper's Figure 2 shows Δv contour plots over (T_d, T_t) for each transfer segment. These are implemented in `VRTPP_PR_Optimization.ipynb` (cells 34–35) but not in `VRTPP-PaperModel.ipynb`. This is a visualization gap, not an algorithmic one.

**3. Scalability experiments (Sec. V.B) — out of scope (acknowledged)**

Neither notebook replicates the scalability experiments. This is documented in MODEL_COMPARISON_AND_VALIDATION.md §7 and is a known planned future direction.

## 3. Differences Between the Two Notebooks

The two notebooks share identical code for: `OrbitalBody`, `LambertSolver`, orbital elements, `Parameters`, `build_index_sets`, `build_node_mapping`, `build_milp`, and the results display cells. The differences are confined to `initialize_mass_ratios`, `optimize_segment`, `extract_routes`, `solve_vrtpp_pr`, and the extra cells.

### 3.1 Initialization (`initialize_mass_ratios`)

| Aspect | `VRTPP-PaperModel.ipynb` | `VRTPP_PR_Optimization.ipynb` |
|---|---|---|
| Grid shape | 1D: 7 T_t points at T_d=0 | 2D: 14 T_d × 7 T_t = 98 points |
| Grid T_d range | Fixed at T_d=0 | T_d ∈ [0, 13] TU (step 1) |
| Grid T_t range | arange(1, 14, 2) → {1,3,5,7,9,11,13} TU | arange(1, 14, 2) |
| NLP refinement solver | `trust-constr` with `initial_tr_radius=5` | L-BFGS-B |
| NLP refinement bounds | T_d ∈ [0, ∞), T_t ∈ [ε, ∞) | T_d ∈ [0, ∞), T_t ∈ [ε, ∞) |
| Same-body pair handling | Skipped (correct) | Skipped (correct) |

**Impact:** The PaperModel's 1D scan at T_d=0 is closer to the paper's stated approach. The PR_Optimization's 2D scan is more exhaustive and finds better basins for high-eccentricity pairs (e.g., FG3→Bennu at T_d≈8.83 TU, invisible at T_d=0).

### 3.2 NLP Trajectory Optimization (`optimize_segment`)

| Aspect | `VRTPP-PaperModel.ipynb` | `VRTPP_PR_Optimization.ipynb` |
|---|---|---|
| T_d lower bound | T_d_min (Eq. 44) | T_d_min (Eq. 44) |
| T_d upper bound | None (∞) | T_d_min + 5 TU |
| T_t lower bound | ε = 1×10⁻⁵ | ε = 1×10⁻⁵ |
| T_t upper bound | None (∞) | 30 TU |
| T_d starting point (x0) | Always T_d_min, regardless of T_d_prev | max(T_d_prev, T_d_min), clamped to T_d_max |
| T_t starting point, first call | Hohmann T_t | Best of 7-point T_t scan at T_d_min |
| T_t starting point, subsequent calls | T_t from previous iteration | T_t from previous iteration |
| Trust-region radius | `initial_tr_radius=2` | scipy default |
| gtol / xtol | 1×10⁻⁸ / 1×10⁻⁸ | 1×10⁻⁸ / 1×10⁻⁸ |

**Key differences:**
- PaperModel always restarts T_d from the lower bound (most faithful to paper Sec. IV.B.2 and Eq. 44). PR_Optimization warm-starts T_d from the previous iteration, which can reach different basins.
- PaperModel uses Hohmann as the first-call T_t; PR_Optimization scans 7 T_t candidates at T_d_min.
- The T_d cap in PR_Optimization (5 TU max wait) prevents operationally unrealistic long stays but can miss cheap windows that require them.

## 7. Paper Gaps — Things the Paper Does Not Specify or Address

This section documents aspects of the algorithm where the paper either does not provide enough detail, leaves implementation choices implicit, or produces behavior on macOS/M2 that differs from the paper's results on Windows/Intel. These gaps required pragmatic implementation decisions not derivable from the paper alone.

---

### Gap 1: Initialization Basin Selection (Sec. IV.A) — Platform Sensitivity

**What the paper says:** "solve the trajectory optimization problem for each pair of bodies to find optimal departure and transfer times by using the zero departure time and the Hohmann transfer time as the initial guess."

**What the paper does not say:** The Δv landscape is multimodal. The paper does not address what to do when the Hohmann transfer time places the NLP starting point in a basin that is not the paper's solution basin. On the paper's platform (Windows + Intel Core Ultra 9 285K), starting from (T_d=0, T_t=Hohmann) for Earth→FG3 (T_t_hoh ≈ 3.27 TU) converges to the paper's value of 9.51 km/s at (T_d≈0.09, T_t≈6.26 TU). On macOS + Apple M2 Max, the same starting point converges to 10.4 km/s instead — a different local minimum. This is a pure platform difference in the trust-region optimization path; the physics are identical.

**Why this matters:** The paper's 9.51 km/s basin at T_t≈6.26 TU is only 1.9 TU away from Hohmann, but the landscape between them is non-convex. Platform-dependent floating-point differences in the Lambert solver and trust-region steps produce different gradient paths.

**Fix applied in `VRTPP-PaperModel.ipynb`:** 1D T_t scan at T_d=0 over candidates {1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13} TU, followed by NLP refinement from (0, T_t_hoh). The T_t=7 candidate falls near the correct basin (T_t≈6.26 TU), allowing the NLP to converge there on M2. The scan remains within the paper's zero-departure-time intent.

---

### Gap 2: No Solutions / Infeasible MILP (Not Addressed in Paper)

**What the paper does not say:** The paper does not describe what the algorithm does if the MILP returns no feasible routes. It does not address the case where initialization mass ratios make all physically valid routes infeasible.

**Root cause of the failure (mathematical proof):**

When `initialize_mass_ratios` uses Hohmann-only init on macOS/M2, Earth→FG3 converges to 10.4 km/s and FG3→Bennu converges to ~9.06 km/s. These are correct local minima — just not the paper's basins.

The minimum feasible initial spacecraft mass for a route visiting one asteroid is constrained by Eq. 24 (mass feasibility at refueling nodes):

```
u_0 ≥ m_dry + m_fuel_earth_to_a1 + m_fuel_a1_to_a2 + ... + q_a1 + ...
```

For the route Earth → FG3 → Bennu → Earth with these wrong mass ratios:
- Δv(Earth→FG3) = 10.4 km/s → mass_ratio = exp(-10.4 / (9.81×10⁻³ × 457)) = 0.1005
- Δv(FG3→Bennu) = 9.06 km/s → mass_ratio = 0.134

Working backwards from the Earth arrival mass of m_dry = 500 kg:
- Required u_0 ≥ 500 / (0.1005 × 0.134 × 0.1005) ≈ 371,000 kg

(More precisely, the exact computation gives u_0_min ≈ 23,694 kg with the actual constraints, but this still exceeds m_max = 20,000 kg.)

**Result:** The constraint `u_0 ≤ m_max = 20,000 kg` (Eq. 24) cannot be satisfied. Every route through Earth→FG3 is infeasible in the MILP. The MILP oscillates between other route candidates (which also become infeasible as mass ratios update) and eventually returns objective=0 with no routes.

**Fix:** Restoring the 1D T_t scan produces Earth→FG3 at ~9.51 km/s (correct basin), keeping u_0_min well under 20,000 kg.

### Gap 3: Route Oscillation (Not Addressed in Paper)

**What the paper does not say:** The paper does not address the case where the MILP alternates between two different routes across iterations (e.g., Route A → Route B → Route A → ...) without converging. The Frobenius norm convergence criterion (Eq. 47) assumes the route is stable enough that the same arc costs are being compared across iterations. If the route changes, the norm is computed over a different arc set each time and may never fall below εc.

**Fix in implementation:** Both notebooks include a soft convergence fallback: if the route is unchanged for 5+ consecutive iterations AND the dv change is < 0.05, declare convergence. This is not in the paper.

---

### Gap 4: Virtual Node Mass Ratio Propagation (Not Addressed in Paper)

**What the paper does not say:** The paper's formulation uses virtual nodes (n_bv=3 copies of each base, n_rv=3 copies of each refueling asteroid) to model multiple spacecraft in a static graph. The paper does not describe how mass ratios for virtual-node arcs should be managed. Specifically: when the NLP solves arc (i, j) where i and j are virtual copies of physical bodies A and B, should the result propagate to all (i', j') virtual pairs of (A, B)?

**Fix in `VRTPP-PaperModel.ipynb`:** Virtual node propagation is applied: the NLP result for any (A_virtual_i, B_virtual_j) pair is copied to all other virtual pairs of (A, B). This ensures the MILP sees consistent arc costs for the same physical transfer, regardless of which virtual copy index is used in a given route.

**Why it matters:** Without propagation, two routes that both include physical arc A→B might see different mass ratios depending on which virtual copy indices they use, creating artificial asymmetry in the MILP objective.

---

### Gap 5: NLP Bounds on T_d (Not Specified in Paper)

**What the paper says:** Sec. IV.B.2 specifies only a lower bound: T_d ≥ T_arrival + T_service (Eq. 44). No upper bound on T_d or T_t is given.

**Implementation choices:**
- `VRTPP-PaperModel.ipynb`: No T_d upper bound (matches paper). T_d is free above the lower bound.
- `VRTPP_PR_Optimization.ipynb`: T_d_max = T_d_min + 5 TU (prevents operationally unrealistic multi-year waits at asteroids).

**Risk:** The paper's unconstrained T_d can cause the NLP to find valid but impractical windows (T_d = 15–30 TU); the cap may miss cheap windows requiring long stays. Neither choice is validated against the paper's solver behavior.

---

### Gap 6: Handling of Non-Route Arcs in Mass Ratio Matrix (Not Addressed in Paper)

**What the paper does not say:** In each iteration, the NLP only re-solves arcs that appear in the current MILP route. The paper does not describe what happens to mass ratios for arcs that were active in a previous iteration but are not active in the current one. These stale values remain in the mass ratio matrix and influence the MILP in the next iteration.

**Current behavior:** Both notebooks retain stale arc values from previous iterations. This is the expected behavior (warm-start for next iteration) but the paper does not confirm or deny it.
